Why we exist

Most projects do not fail on skill. They fail in the gaps.

Nearly every client who comes to us arrives with the same story. A web developer who has never seen the brand guidelines. An editor working from a brief written by someone who left. An ads agency waiting six weeks for a landing page that was never scoped. Everyone is competent. Nothing moves.

We built Pixel Assembly so that the handover between those roles happens inside one team, in one conversation, against one deadline. It is not a new idea — it is how the good in-house teams already work. We just rent it out.

We stay deliberately small. You will not be handed to a junior after the pitch, because there is no pitch team. The person who writes your scope writes your code, or cuts your film, or runs your campaign.

Short history

How we got here.

2023

Started with one contract

Two people taking on work that neither could deliver alone. The first client asked for a website and a month of edits at the same time, and the case for keeping both under one roof made itself.

2024

Video and motion came in-house

Sending edits out was costing a week of round-trips per project. Doing them ourselves cut the turnaround to days and made ongoing short-form work practical.

2025

Paid media joined the stack

Clients kept asking who would run the campaign for the site we had just built. Owning the tracking, the landing page and the creative in one place removed most of the finger-pointing.

2026

Six disciplines, one studio

Social management completed the set. A client can now hand over an entire digital function and deal with one team, one timeline and one contract.
